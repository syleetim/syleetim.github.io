Motivation

Motivation: Beyond UBI?

  • Technology expected to widen gaps in earnings and capital income
  • Renewed interest in UBI: income insurance, but
    1. Taxes work and investment to finance transfers
    2. Insurance may disincentivise work and investments (Daruich and Fernández 2024)
    3. Unpledgeable future cash cannot finance investments today
  • UBC: access to capital, not just income flows
  • SWF: broader sharing of capital income

Lifecycle Overview

  • Young: child investment · Old: asset transfers · Retirement: pensions and annuities

Occupational Choice

\[ \begin{aligned} V_s(a,h,z)&=\mathbb E_{\xi}\!\left[ \max\left\{\bar V_s^W(a,h,z)+\xi_s^W,\; \bar V_s^E(a,h,z)+\zeta_s+\xi_s^E\right\}\right]\\[0.6em] \bar V_s^o(a,h,z)&=\mathbb E_{\epsilon^o} \left[V_s^o(a,h,z,\epsilon^o)\right],\qquad o\in\{W,E\},\quad s\in\{1,2,3\} \end{aligned} \]

  • assets \(a\), human capital \(h\), business ability \(z\)
  • Choose to be worker \(W\) (retiree) or entrepreneur \(E\)
  • \(\xi_s^o\): taste shocks
  • \(\zeta_s\): entrepreneurial preference
  • \(\epsilon^o\): earnings/productivity shock realized after occupation choice

Young Households: Post-Shock Choices

Given occupation \(o\) and realized shock \(\epsilon^o\),

\[ \begin{aligned} V_1^o(a,h,z,\epsilon^o) &=\max_{c,a',e,n}\Bigl\{q_1u(c)-\mathbf 1_{\{o=W\}}v_1(n)+\beta\,\mathbb E_{\eta_h}\bigl[\widetilde V_2(a',h,z,h')\bigr]\Bigr\}\\[0.5em] c+a'+e&=(1+r)a+y^o-T(y^o,a,c)+{\color{red} \omega}\\ y^W&=\psi_1\epsilon^h whn, \qquad h'=H(h,e,\eta_h)\\ y^E&=(1-{\color{red} \kappa})\max_{k,\ell\geq0} \Bigl\{\,{\color{blue}{\epsilon^{z}}} z(k^\alpha\ell^{1-\alpha})^\nu-w\ell-Rk\Bigr\} \end{aligned} \]

  • \(q_1\): family scale · \(v_1(n)\): worker labor disutility · \(\psi_1\): age profile
  • \(T(y,a,c)\): income, capital and consumption taxes
  • \({\color{red} \omega}\): total transfers, including SWF dividends
  • borrowing and collateral constraints

UBC: Pledgeability of Future Transfers

  • \({\color{red}{\Omega_s}}\): present value of transfers from stage \(s\) onward

  • Borrowing and collateral constraints \[ a_{s+1}\geq-\frac{{\color{red}{\phi_h\Omega_{s+1}}}}{1+r}, \qquad k\leq\lambda(a+{\color{red}{\phi_k\Omega_s}}) \]

  • \({\color{red}{\phi_h}}\): pledgeable share for child investment and saving

  • \({\color{red}{\phi_k}}\): pledgeable share for entrepreneurial capital

SWF: Less Distortionary but Crowds Out Capital

  • Public ownership: share \({\color{red} \kappa}\) of entrepreneurial capital \[ (1-{\color{red} \kappa})k\le\lambda(a+\color{red}{\phi_k\Omega_s}) \]

  • Net fund income: business returns minus depreciation and debt service

  • Use of proceeds: universal dividends or lower taxes

  • Corporate firms also employ capital and labor

    • SWF only holds entrepreneurial capital (for now)

Tradeoff: can lower capital returns, leading to ambiguous GE effects

GE channels

Old Age and Retirement

\[ \begin{aligned} \widetilde V_2(a,h,z,h')&= \max_{b\geq0}\left\{V_2(a-b,h,z)+\theta\,\mathbb E_{z'\mid h'}V_1(b,h',z')\right\}\\[0.6em] z'&=Z(h',\eta_z') \end{aligned} \]

  • Old choose transfer \(b\)
    • after observing child’s human capital \(h'\)
    • before child’s business ability and current income shocks
  • \(\eta_z'\): mean-one ability shock
  • Remaining choices symmetric to young, but without child investment
  • Death or Retirement:
    • pension and annuity income; latter eliminates accidental bequests
    • entrepreneurship still possible

Government Budget and SWF Finance

\[ \begin{aligned} T_t+S_t+\Delta B_t&=G_t+N{\color{red} \omega_t}+r_tB_t+\delta K_{g,t}+\Delta K_{g,t}\\[0.6em] K_{g,t}&={\color{red} \kappa_t}\int k_t(x)\,d\mu_t^E(x),\qquad {\color{blue}{B_t=K_{g,t}}} \end{aligned} \]

  • \(\mu_t^E\): entrepreneur population across ages, states and shocks

  • \(T\): taxes · \(S\): fund receipts · \(G\): pensions and other spending

  • Net fund income: \(S_t-(r_t+\delta)K_{g,t}\)
    Universal dividends or lower ordinary taxes

  • For now: immediate and permanent debt rollover of SWF when introduced

Model Implications

UBI vs UBC

  • UBI: Daruich and Fernández (2024) find long-run reduction in education
  • UBC: makes subsidy available when needed for investment

SWF: Returns and Financing

  • Public equity: shared returns, lower private financing needs
  • Constrained firms can earn rental cost of capital \(r+\delta\)
  • Thus net SWF gains can exceed cost, even in GE

Inequality and Mechanisms

  • Wider inequality strengthens case for SWF: constrained bottom, unconstrained top
  • Higher entrepreneurial returns \(\rightarrow\) higher value of collateral
  • Human-capital complementarity \(\rightarrow\) higher return to education investments

Results

Productive Capacity Across Policies

  • Pledgeable future transfers expand financing for child investment

All policy outcomes

Public Ownership and Private Capital

  • UBC: financing access supports expansion of private capital
  • SWF alone: public capital rises, private capital falls slightly

Redistribution Beyond Market Income

  • UBC+SWF and UBC: taxes and transfers compress disposable-income dispersion
  • Market-income inequality changes little despite higher productive activity

Income Equality and Private Wealth Concentration

  • Dividends and fiscal adjustment raise the bottom-half income share
  • More concentrated private wealth need not imply lower welfare

Income and Welfare by Occupation

  • UBC: pledgeable future transfers support investment and earnings
  • SWF alone: weaker entrepreneurial market income; tax relief cushions decline
    • Weaker returns lower entrepreneur share

Market-income changes Impact versus long run

Welfare Gains Across Lifecycle

  • Young utility gains: 63% own life, 37% descendants
  • Old and retired: remaining lifetime

UBC+SWF: Young Cohorts

  • Entry timing changes exposure to dividends, prices and investment opportunities
  • Later entrants inherit an economy with adjusted skills and assets

Impact welfare

UBC+SWF: Prices and Fiscal Flows

  • On impact: labor reallocates from corporate to entrepreneurs \(\Rightarrow\) \(\downarrow\) capital demand
  • Later: \(\uparrow\) human capital and entrepreneurial returns \(\Rightarrow\) \(\uparrow\) capital demand
  • On impact: \(\downarrow r\Rightarrow\) \(\downarrow\)capital-tax receipts \(\Rightarrow\) \(\uparrow\) income tax
  • Later: \(\uparrow\) capital- and consumption-tax receipts \(\Rightarrow\) \(\downarrow\) income tax

Policy and Inheritance

  • Actual: policy outcomes plus changed inherited assets and skills
  • Benchmark: same starting assets, skills and ability distribution
  • Daruich and Fernández (2024): inherited skills and assets account for roughly half of 21.9% welfare loss

Conclusion

  • Investment: pledgeable transfers support education, entry and productive capacity
  • Redistribution: more equal disposable income; gains differ by age and occupation
  • Ownership: UBC+SWF raises public and private capital; private wealth remains concentrated

Inclusive capitalism:

  • UBC opens access to capital
  • SWF shares its returns
